THE PROBLEM

Every team rebuilds the same things. Then their AI rebuilds them again.

The auth module your team perfected two projects ago still exists — buried in that repo, tangled with its dependencies, invisible to search. So the next project writes a new one, and an assistant asked the same question writes a third.

Knowledge nobody can find

Engineering teams lose their best work because it gets buried in old repos, stripped of the context explaining why it was built.

AI that regenerates

AI assistants default to generating new code from scratch, which increases your maintenance burden and introduces subtle bugs.

Why this matters now

With AI making code generation free, the new bottleneck is review and maintenance; reusing proven pieces is the only way to scale.

The knowledge was never lost.
It was never findable.

HOW IT WORKS

Capture once. Find it anywhere. Add it in one command.

Three verbs, one binary. Everything lives in a git folder on your machine called the Vault — no server, no account.

1

Capture

Save the piece to your Vault. The CLI extracts the files, asks you for context, and generates the summaries.

HUMAN
$ brico capture
Select files: [src/auth/*]
Captured auth@0.1.0
2

Find

Search by name, capability, or meaning. The local SQLite index finds it instantly.

HUMAN / AI ASSISTANT
$ brico search "auth"
auth@0.2.0 (proven)
3

Add

Copy the files into your working directory and get the exact wiring steps.

HUMAN
$ brico add auth
Copied to src/auth/
Done.

FOR AI ASSISTANTS

Your assistant gets a librarian instead of the whole library.

brico mcp exposes the Vault over the Model Context Protocol. Claude, Cursor, IDE agents, internal copilots — any MCP-capable assistant can search your pieces and recommend reuse before writing anything new.

search_pieces(query, kind?)

Finds pieces by meaning or capability.

get_piece(name, level)

Fetches the exact detail level needed (card, surface, summary, source).

plan_reuse(task)

Returns a recommended assembly of existing pieces.

get_relations(name, kind?)

Navigates the dependency graph.

FAQ

So what is BricoWerx, in one breath? +
A version-controlled library of your organisation's proven building blocks, with the wiring and the reasoning attached, readable by humans and by AI.
Is it a code generator? +
No. It does not generate new code. It finds and retrieves code your team has already written, reviewed, and proven in production.
Is it a package registry like npm? +
No. It's designed for the "glue code" and internal modules that are too specific or too numerous to publish as formal packages. You copy the pieces into your project and modify them as needed.
Do I need a server, an account, or a database? +
No. The Vault is just a git repository on your machine. BricoWerx uses a local SQLite index for search. You don't need a central server unless your team grows extremely large.
Which stacks does it support today? +
It works with any text-based files. The CLI has language-specific understanding for TypeScript, Go, Python, and Rust, allowing it to extract dependencies automatically.
Where does the name come from? +
From the French word bricolage (/bri.ko.laj/): making something new from whatever is already at hand, and letting the joins show. We apply it to engineering knowledge in the AI era.
